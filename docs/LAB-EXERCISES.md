# Help Desk Lab Exercises

Use fictional names, email addresses, device names, and services. Create one ticket per scenario in osTicket. Practice writing clear updates and recording evidence.

## Exercise 1: Account lockout

**Requester:** Jordan Lee

**Issue:** The fictional HR portal rejects the user's sign-in after several attempts.

**Practice:** Confirm identity using the lab's stated process, collect the error and approximate time, check whether other users are affected, assign an owner, and give a realistic update. Record the resolution without requesting or storing the user's password.

## Exercise 2: Network drive unavailable

**Requester:** Sam Rivera

**Issue:** The fictional Finance share is unavailable after a laptop restart.

**Practice:** Capture the exact path, device name, start time, and whether colleagues are affected. Determine impact and urgency, document troubleshooting steps, and explain any escalation.

## Exercise 3: Hardware request

**Requester:** Alex Morgan

**Issue:** A keyboard has intermittent key failures.

**Practice:** Capture the asset tag, symptoms, business impact, and whether a workaround exists. Assign the ticket to the appropriate queue, document replacement approval if your lab policy calls for it, and record the handoff.

## Exercise 4: New team folder access

**Requester:** Taylor Chen

**Target user:** Casey Patel

**System:** Fictional project file share

**Requested role:** Read-only

**Business reason:** Casey is joining the project team for a short assignment.

**Practice:** Verify the requester and target identity, check that the business reason and requested role are specific, identify the manager and data owner as approvers, record approval evidence in an internal note, and request an end date. Keep the ticket pending until all approvals are recorded. Close it as simulated after documenting the decision.

Do not grant access to real systems. This lab records the approval process only.

## Exercise 5: Onboarding checklist

**Requester:** A fictional hiring manager

**Target user:** A fictional new starter

**Practice:** Use a checklist in the ticket for account, device, and application requests. Separate each access item and approver. Do not include actual credentials or personal data.

## Completion checklist

- Every ticket has a concise subject, category, priority, requester, and clear description.
- Priority is justified by impact and urgency.
- Updates distinguish user-visible replies from internal notes.
- Work performed and handoffs are recorded in order.
- Access tickets identify requester, target, system, role, business need, approvers, evidence, and expiry.
- No passwords, secrets, or real personal data appear in tickets.
- Tickets are resolved only after documenting the outcome and communicating it.
